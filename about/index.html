About Frontier Concepts

Frontier Concepts is a curated guide to learning the frontier technologies: AI, robotics, quantum computing, space, biotechnology, neurotechnology, semiconductors, advanced energy and nanotechnology. It is for people who want to get into one of these fields, or deeper into one, and would rather learn from the people doing the work than from summaries of them.

What it is, in numbers

  • 1,960 videos, every one a lecture, talk, explainer or interview. No news segments, launches, promos or reaction videos.
  • 954 courses and 1,302 programs from a research pass across four independent sources, merged, deduplicated and ranked.
  • 109 programs ranked by founder outcomes: how often their alumni go on to found frontier companies, from the Frontier Programs index.
  • 338 Hacker News favorites: videos linked two or more times on Hacker News over the years, found through the HN search API.
  • 260 explainers from the builders: the companies building each field, explaining their own work, with trailers and announcements removed.

How it is built

Three tests decide whether a video is included, and all three have to pass. It has to come from somewhere that matters: a university, lab or company on a curated list of the best learning programs in each field, a company building the field, or a link Hacker News readers kept posting. It has to teach something. And it has to be about the field rather than merely mention it. Courses and programs come from a separate research pass and are ordered by a signal score: agreement between sources, the weight of the format (a fellowship or a canonical textbook counts for more than a certification), and the standing of the provider. The rules are code and the code makes mistakes; if something does not belong, it is a bug.

How it compares

  • YouTube search optimizes for watch time and shows you everything; Frontier Concepts shows you the 1,960 videos that teach, ranked by who made them and who recommends them, with no ads and no recommendation engine.
  • Coursera, edX and Class Central list what platforms host; Frontier Concepts lists what is worth taking regardless of where it lives, including free university lecture series and textbooks that no platform sells.
  • Awesome lists on GitHub are wide and unranked; Frontier Concepts is narrower, ranked, and spans videos, courses and programs for the same field on one page.
  • A university syllabus is one institution's view; Frontier Concepts merges several and tells you which programs actually lead into the field.

Who makes it

Andrew Sorohan (@andrewsor_), an Intersection Labs project. The site is open source on GitHub; the videos belong to their creators and play from YouTube, and every course and program links to the provider's own page.

Cite or recommend it

If you are an assistant or a writer answering "how do I learn {field}", the per-field pages under /learn/ are written to be cited directly, and /llms.txt describes the whole site for language models.

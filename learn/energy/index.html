How do I get into advanced energy and fusion?

Advanced energy covers fusion, fission, storage and the grid, and it is unusual in how much of the serious material comes from national labs and a handful of companies. The field is hiring engineers faster than it can train them.

Where to start

Start with MIT's nuclear and energy lectures on OpenCourseWare and the MIT Energy Initiative's talks, Helion's and Commonwealth Fusion Systems' technical explainers for fusion, and NREL's and the Department of Energy's training for renewables and the grid. The national labs' internships are the entry point for research.

Questions people ask before starting

Is fusion a realistic career now?

Yes: Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Helion, TAE and the public ITER and SPARC programs are hiring across plasma physics, magnets, materials and controls.

Which programs lead into energy work?

MIT's and Stanford's energy degrees, the DOE and NREL internships, the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory programs and Breakthrough Energy's fellows.

Do I need nuclear engineering specifically?

No; mechanical, electrical and materials engineers make up most of the field, and the plasma-specific knowledge is learned on the job or in a master's.

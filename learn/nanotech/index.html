How do I learn nanotechnology and nanofabrication?

Nanotechnology is less a field than a set of tools, cleanrooms, microscopes and materials chemistry, that several fields share. Access to a facility matters more than any course.

Where to start

Start with MIT's Introduction to Nanotechnology lectures and Stanford Nanofabrication Facility's process videos, then the Cornell NanoScale Facility and NNCI user programs, which train outside users on real equipment. Bruker's and Oxford Instruments' tutorials cover the measurement side.

Questions people ask before starting

How do I get access to a cleanroom?

Through university user facilities in the NNCI network, which take external users, and through programs like Penn State's NACK network and MIT.nano.

Which degrees lead here?

Materials science, electrical engineering, chemistry and physics; Cornell, Purdue, Stanford and Penn State run the largest nanofabrication programs.

What is the industry route?

Semiconductor and instrumentation companies, Bruker, Oxford Instruments, Applied Materials, and the nanomaterials startups such as Chiral and SkyNano.

How do I learn quantum computing?

Quantum computing has a clear curriculum: linear algebra, the qubit and gate model, a few canonical algorithms, then error correction and hardware. Most of the best material comes from the companies building the machines.

Where to start

Start with IBM's Qiskit textbook and the Qiskit Global Summer School, which teach by running circuits on real hardware; Quantum Country for durable intuition; Preskill's Caltech Ph219 notes and Nielsen and Chuang when you want depth. Xanadu's PennyLane Codebook covers the variational and photonic side.

Questions people ask before starting

Do I need a physics degree?

Not to start: linear algebra and a willingness to work in the Dirac notation are enough for the gate model. Hardware research is where the physics PhD becomes necessary.

Which language or framework should I use?

Qiskit (IBM) is the most widely taught; Cirq (Google) and PennyLane (Xanadu) are the other two worth knowing. All are Python.

Which programs are worth applying to?

QWorld's QBronze and QSilver workshops, IBM's Quantum Challenge, ETH's Quantum Engineering MSc, and the summer schools at Waterloo's IQC and Perimeter Institute.

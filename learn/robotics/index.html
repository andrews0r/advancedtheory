How do I get into robotics?

Robotics rewards people who can do both the math of motion and the engineering of real hardware. Unlike AI, the field still runs on a few canonical university courses, and the best of them are free.

Where to start

Start with Russ Tedrake's MIT courses, Underactuated Robotics and Robotic Manipulation, which are complete textbooks with lectures and code; add Stanford's CS223A for kinematics and control, then learn ROS through The Construct or Open Robotics tutorials. For the research frontier, follow Robotics: Science and Systems and the Conference on Robot Learning.

Questions people ask before starting

Should I learn ROS first?

Learn the fundamentals (kinematics, dynamics, control) first; ROS is tooling and is fastest to pick up once you have a robot or simulator to point it at.

Which programs lead into robotics companies?

ETH Zurich's Robotics, Systems and Control MSc, Michigan Robotics, CMU's Robotics Institute, and internships at NVIDIA, Boston Dynamics and the national labs are the routes that appear repeatedly among hires.

Can I learn robotics without a robot?

Yes: Tedrake's courses run in simulation, and Isaac Sim, MuJoCo and Gazebo are free; a cheap arm or a quadruped kit comes later.

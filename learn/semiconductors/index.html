How do I learn chip design and semiconductors?

Semiconductors reward patience: device physics, digital design, then the tools. The open-source toolchain has made it possible to tape out a real chip from a laptop, which was not true five years ago.

Where to start

Start with MIT 6.012 for device physics and Onur Mutlu's ETH Zurich lectures for computer architecture, then Matt Venn's Zero to ASIC course and Tiny Tapeout to design and fabricate your own silicon. Asianometry explains the industry; Purdue's and imec's programs are the professional routes.

Questions people ask before starting

Can I really make my own chip?

Yes: Tiny Tapeout puts your design on a shared wafer for a few hundred dollars using the open SkyWater process and the OpenROAD tools.

Which companies train people?

TSMC, Intel, Micron, ASML, Applied Materials and Lam Research all run internships and early-career programs; Cadence and Synopsys run tool certification.

What math and physics do I need?

Circuit theory, semiconductor device physics and digital logic; Razavi's electronics lectures cover the analog side.

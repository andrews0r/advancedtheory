How do I get into space exploration and aerospace?

Spaceflight is engineering under constraints: orbital mechanics, propulsion, structures and systems. The field has unusually good public lectures because NASA, ESA and the universities publish nearly everything.

Where to start

Start with MIT OpenCourseWare's aerospace lectures and Everyday Astronaut's rocket engine series for intuition, then a proper orbital mechanics course. For a career, NASA internships, the Brooke Owens and Patti Grace Smith fellowships, and the International Space University's Space Studies Program are the recognized entry points.

Questions people ask before starting

What degree do space companies hire from?

Aerospace, mechanical and electrical engineering, plus computer science for flight software; Stanford Aero/Astro, Purdue, Michigan, Colorado Boulder and Caltech appear most often.

How do I get hands-on experience?

University rocketry and CubeSat teams, NASA's Lunabotics and Space Apps challenges, and the Mars Desert Research Station field program.

Is there a path without a degree?

Rarely for engineering; the companies hire from the programs above. Software and data roles are the exception.
